Northwind Grid AG Annual Report 2025

Fiscal year from 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025. This page is a test fixture with known text for the web report provider tests of open foresight.

Letter from the board

Northwind Grid AG makes switchgear, transformers and grid control software for distribution system operators in Europe. Revenue rose by six percent to 9.4 billion euros, and the order backlog reached 21 billion euros at the end of the year. The board proposes a dividend of 2.10 euros per share. Free cash flow was 1.1 billion euros. The group employs 41,000 people in 28 countries and runs factories in Germany, Poland, Spain and India.

Utilities connected more heat pumps, chargers and rooftop solar systems to their low voltage networks than in any year before, and they bought the equipment and software that lets them do so without building new lines. That demand carried every segment of the group through the year.

Smart Infrastructure segment

The segment sells medium voltage switchgear, protection relays and the grid control software that distribution system operators use to balance demand and local solar generation. Revenue was 4.8 billion euros and the profit margin was 14.2 percent. Orders for grid automation software grew by 19 percent, led by utilities in Germany and the Netherlands that are adding flexible connections for heat pumps and chargers.

The segment opened a relay factory in Wroclaw and doubled its software team in Madrid. Prices for copper and electrical steel rose during the year, and the segment passed most of the increase on to customers through price clauses in framework contracts.

Mobility and rail signalling segment

The segment supplies interlockings, train control equipment and maintenance services to rail operators in Europe and Asia. Revenue was 2.9 billion euros and new orders reached 3.6 billion euros, so the backlog grew to 11 billion euros. The largest order of the year was a signalling contract for a regional network in northern Spain.

The segment margin fell to 8.1 percent because two fixed price projects ran over budget. The board expects the margin to recover in the next fiscal year as these projects finish and the new digital interlocking platform reaches volume production.
